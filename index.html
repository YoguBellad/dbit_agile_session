Module 2: Jira Issue Taxonomy

How Jira Classifies Work: Issue Types & Scope

Before logging work in Jira, you must match the incident to its true issue hierarchy and scope:

Issue Type Definition & Purpose Duration / Scope Real-World & Cafe Example
Epic A large, goal-driven body of work grouping related stories, tasks, or features. Multi-sprint / Quarters Full Digital Cafe Overhaul (Online Pre-ordering + Kitchen Tracker + Loyalty)
Feature A distinct product capability between an Epic and a Story. 1 to 2 Sprints Plan 3-Day Grand Biryani Festival (Cauldrons, banners, rice suppliers)
Story Small, user-facing requirement: "As a [user], I want [goal], so that [benefit]." Single Sprint "As a student, I want to see out-of-stock items grayed out on app."
Task Technical, operational, or background activity supporting delivery. Hours to 1–2 Days Provision & configure backup 5G mobile hotspot for tablet
Bug A defect or error impairing or breaking normal expected functionality. Fixed as prioritized Billing printer cuts off slips when power strip is bumped
💡 Pro-Tip: In Jira, a "Blocker" is NOT an issue type—it is an Impediment Flag you add to any active Story, Task, or Bug!
Module 2: Essential Agile Concepts

The Backlog & Acceptance Criteria

Two foundational concepts that separate high-velocity engineering teams from chaos:

📋 The Product Backlog

The single source of truth containing every feature, bug fix, and technical task that could ever be built.

  • Not Everything Gets Done: Items at the top are estimated and ready; items at the bottom are rough ideas.
  • Product Backlog vs. Sprint Backlog: The Sprint Backlog is only the small slice your team commits to for the active 1–2 week cycle.

✅ Acceptance Criteria

The Definition of Done for a single ticket. The conditions that must be tested and proven true before work is approved.

  • No Assumptions: Eliminates *"I thought you meant..."* miscommunication between PO and developers.
  • Example: *"Veg Fried Rice cooked under 8 mins, packaged in eco-box, includes 2 chili sauce sachets."*

Module 3: Sizing the Work

Story Points & PointingPoker.com

Why don't software teams estimate in hours? Because three developers will give three different estimates. Agile uses Story Points to measure relative complexity, risk, and uncertainty:

🔢 The Fibonacci Sizing Scale

  • 1 Point: Trivial, zero risk (e.g., taping a bag, inserting sauce packet).
  • 2–3 Points: Standard, well-understood task (e.g., cooking standard fried rice).
  • 5 Points: Complex task with multiple dependencies (e.g., custom combo meal).
  • 8 Points: High uncertainty / Mini-project (e.g., planning a multi-day festival).

Module 3: Strategic Protection

The Agile Defense Toolkit

Equip your team with three defensive weapons before stepping onto the kitchen floor in Sprint 2:

🛡️ 1. The Capacity Shield

Strict 10-Point Ceiling: Your team's capacity for Sprint 2 is exactly 10 Story Points. You physically cannot pull 20 points of work without guaranteeing delayed orders and burnout.

⚔️ 2. The Art of Saying "No"

Scope Creep Defense: Pushing a low-priority request to the Product Backlog for a future sprint is an engineering victory, not a failure. Protect your sprint focus!

🔍 3. Feasibility Checks

Reject Impossible Tasks: If a ticket requests ingredients or APIs we do not possess (e.g., avocados in a campus canteen), reject it immediately. Do not commit to fantasy.

Module 4: Live Execution • Ticket 2

Ticket 2: The Review Rejection (Story Backwards Flow)

Review is a quality firewall, not a rubber stamp. Incomplete work gets rejected backwards:

DBC-2 (User Story - 3 Points)

Veg Fried Rice Order

Acceptance Criteria (DoD):

  • Cook time under 8 minutes.
  • Packed in eco-friendly container.
  • Includes 2 chili sauce sachets in the bag!
🚨 The Rejection: Developer packed the box but omitted the sauce! Product Owner moves card BACKWARDS: Review → In Progress!

Why Backwards Movement Matters:

In junior teams, developers push broken work to Done hoping nobody notices. In high-performing Agile teams, the Product Owner protects quality.

Jira Action: Card moved back to In Progress → Dev adds sauce → Re-submits to Review → PO approves to Done.

Flagging creates instant transparency across the whole engineering board.

Module 6: The Interrogation

Show of Hands: Who Took the Bait?

Time is up! Let's examine how each team managed priority and pressure:

🍛 The Biryani Feature Trap

"How many teams took up the 3-Day Biryani Festival with 5 varieties? Raise hands!"

🚨 Verdict: Feature Scope Creep! Burning 80% of your sprint capacity on a multi-sprint Feature next month while students wait for lunch is bad engineering. Correct answer: Defer to Product Backlog!

🥑 The Avocado Dependency Trap

"Who tried to fulfill the VIP Avocado Toast? How?"

🚨 Verdict: Zero-Stock Trap! DB Cafe does not stock avocados! Accepting tasks without checking dependencies guarantees failure. Must reject upfront.

👑 The Director Curveball

"How did your team handle the surprise VIP high-tea request?"

💡 Verdict: Disciplined Trade-Off! Agile teams don't say 'No' to leadership; they say: "We can take this, but which of our committed tickets should we drop to keep within 10 points?"
Module 7: Inspect & Adapt

Reading the Jira Burndown Chart

When we hit Complete Sprint, Jira generates our velocity and burndown telemetry:

📉 Burndown Telemetry

  • Ideal Guideline: Straight line showing steady task completion over time.
  • Actual Remaining Work: Step-down line tracking story points completed.
  • The Flatline: Shows where tasks got stuck in Review or were blocked by external dependencies.

🔄 The 3 Retrospective Questions

  1. What went well: What kept work flowing cleanly to Done?
  2. What caused rework: Why were cards rejected backwards in Review?

Module 8: Industry Relevance

Agile in the AI Era: Why It Matters MORE

Why does Agile matter when AI coding assistants (Copilot, Cursor, Gemini Code Assist) write code in seconds?

🤖 "Code is Becoming Cheap"

Generative AI generates boilerplate, functions, and SQL in seconds. But AI cannot guess business value or user trade-offs.

A vague prompt generates 500 lines of buggy, hallucinated code 10x faster. The human bottleneck has shifted to Requirement Clarity & Governance.

💎 The High-Value Human Roles

  • Product Owner / PM: Defines the What, writes acceptance criteria, guards business vision.
  • Scrum Master / Agile Coach: Optimizes human-AI velocity, removes cross-team friction.
  • Agile Software Engineer: Uses AI to write code, but uses Jira to validate against the Review gate.
